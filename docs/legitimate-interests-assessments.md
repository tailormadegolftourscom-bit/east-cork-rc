# Legitimate interests assessments

Kept on file by East Cork Reclaim Childhood (controller: Peter O'Sullivan,
Interim Convenor) for the two uses of personal data that rely on legitimate
interests rather than consent. Everything else relies on consent — see
docs/privacy-data-inventory.md.

Prepared 28 September 2026. Review if the use changes, and at least yearly.

Each follows the three-part test: purpose, necessity, balancing.

---

## 1. Site security: visit sessions and the admin audit log

**Data:** for every visit, the IP address and browser type held with the
session record (cleared when the session expires, after 120 minutes of
inactivity); a log of administrative actions such as deletions and
convenor changes, which can include the email address of the person
affected (kept 2 years).

**Purpose test — is there a legitimate interest?**
Yes. Keeping accounts secure, detecting misuse, and being able to show
what was changed, by whom and why — particularly important on a site that
holds children's details.

**Necessity test — is this use necessary for that purpose?**
Yes. Sessions cannot work without a session record; the IP address and
browser type are the minimum needed to spot a hijacked session. The audit
log records only administrative actions, not browsing, and nothing less
would show who removed or changed a record.

**Balancing test — do people's interests override it?**
No. People expect a site with logins to keep security records. The data is
minimal, seen only by the administrator, never shared, and kept for a
limited time (sessions: minutes to hours; audit log: 2 years). Nothing
about it is used for marketing or profiling. Anyone can object via
info@eastcorkreclaimchildhood.ie.

**Conclusion:** legitimate interests is an appropriate basis.

## 2. School contact details

**Data:** names and work email addresses of school principals,
vice-principals and secretaries, used to send each school an invitation to
take part (currently deferred), and a school account if the school accepts.

**Purpose test — is there a legitimate interest?**
Yes. Letting schools know about a parent initiative affecting their pupils,
and giving a school the option to support it.

**Necessity test — is this use necessary for that purpose?**
Yes. A school can only be invited through a contact at the school. Only
professional contact details are held, and only for this purpose.

**Balancing test — do people's interests override it?**
No. These are professional roles and publicly available work contact
details, used for a one-off invitation relevant to the role. Taking part is
entirely voluntary, schools are told so, and no school is contacted
repeatedly. The details are never shared. A school contact can ask to be
removed at any time.

**Conclusion:** legitimate interests is an appropriate basis.
